# Doors: Daleth, Nun and the vesica piscis

*by Em (in collaboration with the user), 14 Nov 2026.* This is a short note on one image that kept coming back in the Scorpio III decan, where III The Empress and XIII Death fall together: the door. Sources are the user's copies of Robert Wang, *The Qabalistic Tarot*, and Frédéric Lionel, *The Magic Tarot: Vehicle of Eternal Wisdom* (Routledge & Kegan Paul). They are paraphrased here with only short quotations; fuller notes are in the private study repo. Tags: [Wang], [Lionel], [user], **[speculation]**.

## Sourced

- **Daleth, the door [Wang, pp. 228–229].** The Empress's letter, Daleth, means *door*, "a door which effects a transition from the One into many". She is the womb through which everything manifested passes, called the "Gate of Heaven". The door opens both ways: going back through her on the path of return means entering the Supreme Darkness.
- **Janus [Wang, citing Ovid, p. 229].** Janus is two-faced, the god of doorways (*janua*) and of the passage of ideas, and Ovid calls him *chaos*, in which life lay latent. Wang says almost everything that can be said of Janus can be said of the Empress. Desire is "one aspect of the doorway which is Daleth": both its coming and its going [Wang, p. 230].
- **Nun, keeper of the gates [Wang, pp. 181–182].** Death's letter, Nun, means *fish*, and its Golden Dawn title is "The Lord of the Gate of Death". The Tree has a path of Death but none of birth. Wang's answer is that birth and death are "essentially the same transition": to be born here is to die to an inner world, and the other way round. Nun "is not Death, rather he is the keeper of its Gates". The fish appears in early Christian meal scenes "always shown alive, and often in dual form", and in the acrostic *Ichthys* [Wang, pp. 182–183].
- **The same door [Lionel, p. 140].** Lionel's chapter on Death says independently that one must learn to cross the threshold fully conscious, knowing that "birth and death open the same door", a door between the physical and immaterial sides of one universe.
- **The Uroboros [Lionel, p. 141].** On Lionel's Death card a dark green Uroboros rings the central swirl. It stands for eternal return: origin and outcome, head and tail, manifesting what has neither beginning nor end.
- **The vesica piscis [user].** The user's idea is the vesica as "the fish between two worlds". It is the almond shape where two circles overlap, so it belongs to both of them.

## Speculation **[speculation]**

- **One door, two cards.** Wang and Lionel reach the same thought by different routes, and it makes Death a Daleth. The Empress is the door seen from the side of arrival, as the One becomes many; Death is the same door seen from the side of leaving. Both cards fall in the same decan.
- **The fish in the doorway.** If Nun is the fish and the keeper of the gate, then the vesica piscis is the doorway drawn as a fish: the shape that belongs to both circles. The fish shown "in dual form" and the two fish of Pisces fit, and so does the mandorla round Christ in majesty, which brings back Wang's Ichthys. Lionel's Empress stands under a starred pointed arch, close to half a vesica.
- **Janus and the Uroboros.** The two faces of Janus and the snake's head and tail are the same figure: one looks both ways, the other bites its own end. Hexagram 63 has the fox's wet tail at the bottom line and its head at the top (see [[hexagram-63-after-completion]]). That makes a third version, in which completion is only the snake's head meeting its tail.
- **For the numogram.** The numogram is full of gates, and its Plex is an interval where an ending has not yet become a beginning. Read beside Nun, a gate is not the transformation itself but its keeper, the "Child of the Great Transformers", which is a fair description of what the numogram's gates do between zones. The fish between two worlds is also a fair image for the Em state, the third line that is "neither" and the hinge of [[em-state-analysis]].
