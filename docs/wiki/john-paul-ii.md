# St John Paul II (Karol Wojtyła, 1920–2005)

Background for the reading of 22 October 2026, his feast (an optional memorial in England and Wales). The day's figures are **Justice** (Libra, tarot XI) and hexagram **47 困 Kun, "Oppression/Exhaustion"**. This page is historical research, not numogram material. Solidarity and his opposition to communism are taken as known. Quotations are copied from fetched text. Items marked *(search excerpt)* come from search-result extracts, not from full pages I read.

## Under occupation (1939–45)

1. **Quarry and chemical works.** "from 1940 to 1944 Wojtyła variously worked as a messenger for a restaurant, a manual labourer in a limestone quarry and for the Solvay chemical factory, to avoid deportation to Germany" (Wikipedia). Wikipedia also says a lorry accident in the quarry left him "with one shoulder higher than the other and a permanent stoop".
2. **Orphaned by twenty.** His mother died in 1929, his brother Edmund (a doctor) of scarlet fever, and his father in 1941. He later said: "At twenty, I had already lost all the people I loved."
3. **A clandestine seminary.** In October 1942 he "knocked on the door of the Bishop's Palace" and began courses in Sapieha's "clandestine underground seminary". On "Black Sunday", 6 August 1944, the Gestapo rounded up young men. He hid "in the basement of his uncle's house at 10 Tyniecka Street" (Wikipedia).
4. **The Rhapsodic Theatre.** Mieczysław Kotlarczyk founded this underground "theatre of the word" in Kraków on 22 August 1941. It staged seven premieres and 20 performances in private flats; the first was Słowacki's *Król-Duch* *(search excerpt: Archiwum Narodowe w Krakowie; Kotlarczyk article, doi 10.15633/pch.3365)*. The second source quotes his memoir *Gift and Mystery*: secrecy was essential, "otherwise we risked serious punishment from the occupying forces, even deportation to the concentration camps."
5. **Edith Zierer.** In January 1945 he carried a 14-year-old Jewish girl who had escaped a labour camp to a train and stayed with her as far as Kraków. "She later credited Wojtyła with saving her life that day" (Wikipedia, which cites secondary sources).

## Poet, actor, pseudonyms

- He published literary work under **"Andrzej Jawień" and "Stanisław Andrzej Gruda"**, "to distinguish his literary from his religious writings" (Wikipedia). To his student hiking groups he was **"Wujek"** ("Uncle"), so that outsiders would not realise a priest was with them.

## Warsaw, 2 June 1979

- The homily in Victory Square ends: "Let your Spirit descend. Let your Spirit descend. and renew the face of the earth, the face of this land." (Vatican text; the punctuation is as published.) In the same homily he knelt at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier to venerate "every seed that falls into the earth and dies", including "the seed of hard daily toil, with the sweat of one's brow, in the fields, the workshop, the mine", and he named those who died "within the walls of the Warsaw ghetto".

## 13 May 1981 and its afterlives

1. **The shooting.** Mehmet Ali Ağca fired at 17:17 in St Peter's Square. The Pope "lost nearly three-quarters of his blood" (Wikipedia). Two bystanders, Ann Odre and Rose Hall, were also hit.
2. **Forgiveness and the prison visit.** In 1983 he visited Ağca at Rebibbia and said afterwards: "I spoke to him as a brother whom I have pardoned and who has my complete trust." Ağca was pardoned in 2000 "at the Pope's request". In 2014 he laid white roses on the tomb.
3. **Fatima.** The attack fell on the anniversary of the first Fatima apparition, 13 May. The Pope said that "motherly protection and care ... turned out to be stronger than the deadly bullet." According to the Shrine, the bullet was "offered by him to the Shrine on March 26, 1984" and set into Our Lady's crown in 1989 (fatima.pt). In 2000 Cardinal Sodano linked the "Bishop clothed in white" of the Third Secret to the attack.
4. **Motives are still unresolved.** Ağca's statements contradicted one another. The Bulgarian Antonov was acquitted in 1986. The Mitrokhin Commission blamed Soviet military intelligence; its critics called the "Bulgarian connection" disinformation. *Treat any single explanation as unproven.*

## Justice, work, mercy (texts)

- **Laborem Exercens (1981)** teaches "the principle of the priority of labour over capital". Work is "for man" and not man "for work". It praises worker solidarity, which arose against a system "that cried to heaven for vengeance". A just wage is "the concrete means of verifying the justice of the whole socioeconomic system".
- **Sollicitudo Rei Socialis (1987)** and **Centesimus Annus (1991)** complete the social trilogy. *Not fetched; listed from Wikipedia only.*
- **Dives in Misericordia (1980)**: Wikipedia says it presents divine mercy as "the greatest feature of God". *Not fetched directly.* He beatified and canonised Faustina Kowalska; he died on the vigil of Divine Mercy Sunday, was beatified on that feast (2011) and canonised on it (2014).
- **Salvifici Doloris (1984)** is close to a commentary on Kun. It reads Job against his friends' doctrine that suffering is always "punishment for sin". Job's suffering is "suffering without guilt", and reading it through justice alone "seems to trivialize and impoverish the concept of justice". It names "the virtue of perseverance in bearing whatever disturbs and causes harm". It ends: "We ask precisely you who are weak to become a source of strength for the Church and humanity."
- **Day of Pardon, 12 March 2000.** "Let us forgive and ask forgiveness!" He asked pardon "for the divisions which have occurred among Christians, for the violence some have used in the service of the truth". Wikipedia counts "over 100" papal apologies: Galileo, the slave trade, women, Catholic silence during the Holocaust, and others.

## Body, illness, relics

- His Parkinson's was confirmed in 2001 and acknowledged by the Vatican in 2003 (Wikipedia). He kept travelling. His last words were "Pozwólcie mi odejść do domu Ojca" ("Allow me to depart to the house of the Father").
- **The blood vials.** His physician drew four vials in his last illness, in case he needed a transfusion. None was needed. Two went to Dziwisz in Kraków and two stayed at the Bambino Gesù hospital; one of those was exposed at the beatification *(search excerpt: NCR, BBC)*. Dziwisz called blood and hair "relics of the first degree". A blood relic was stolen from Spoleto Cathedral in September 2020 (Wikipedia).
- **"Santo subito!"** was heard at the funeral. Benedict XVI waived the five-year wait. The first approved miracle was the cure of a French nun from Parkinson's, the same disease he had.

## Criticisms (stated fairly)

- **McCarrick.** The Vatican's own 2020 report found that he was warned in 1999. He first decided not to appoint McCarrick, then appointed him to Washington after McCarrick wrote, "I have never had sexual relations with any person". The report suggests that John Paul was influenced by his Polish experience of communist "spurious allegations against bishops" (Wikipedia, summarising the report).
- **Maciel.** Complaints reached the Vatican from 1976 and again in 1989. Wikipedia: "The allegations were ignored by John Paul II who continued his praise of Maciel." Maciel was disciplined only under Benedict XVI, in 2006. *Who decided what in 1999 is "not publicly known" (Wikipedia).*
- **Poland, 2023.** TVN24's *Franciszkańska 3* (Marcin Gutowski) alleged that as Archbishop of Kraków he knew of abusing priests and moved them. The Sejm passed a resolution defending his "good name", 271 votes to 43. In 2024 the broadcasting regulator KRRiT fined TVN 550,000 zł, criticising the programme's reliance on secret-police (SB) files *(search excerpts: TVN24, rp.pl, krrit.pl)*. *The archival evidence is still disputed. The KRRiT ruling was itself seen as politically aligned. Ekke Overbeek's book* Maxima culpa *(2023) is not checked here.*
- In 2002 he told the US cardinals, "there is no place in the priesthood and religious life for those who would harm the young." Critics, quoted on Wikipedia, say this came after "decades of inaction".

## Speculative reading (not sourced fact)

*Everything below is interpretation.*

- **Kun's image.** Wilhelm/Baynes: "There is no water in the lake: the image of Exhaustion. Thus the superior man stakes his life on following his will." The judgment reads: "When one has something to say, it is not believed" (quoted via DeKorne's comparison page). Legge's note explains the character as "a tree within an enclosure", "not allowed to spread its branches". This fits occupied Kraków: a theatre "of the word" in sealed flats, a seminary in hiding, a stage name. Words had to go underground because public speech was not believed, or not permitted.
- **Lake over water.** The upper trigram Tui (lake, mouth, joy) sits over Kan (water, danger). In 1981 the danger was literal: he lost most of his blood. The vials of liquid blood, kept from clotting by an anti-coagulant, could be read as the lake's missing water preserved in glass.
- **Justice.** Laborem Exercens makes a just wage the scale on which a whole system is weighed. Salvifici Doloris refuses to make suffering a verdict of justice; it argues that justice alone "trivializes" itself. Card XI holds the scales. Here they are weighed twice: once as social justice, once against the theodicy of Job's friends.
- **Words not believed, twice.** Abuse survivors went unheard by his administration (Vaca's letters, McCarrick). McCarrick's own denial was believed. Kun's warning about trusting words cuts in both directions, and the page should not soften that.
- **The fifth line.** "Nose and feet cut off ... Joy comes softly" (Wilhelm, via DeKorne). In his last years his speech and walking failed in public. Whether this reading fits is for the user to decide.
