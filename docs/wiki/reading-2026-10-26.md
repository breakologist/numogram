## Three mouths

**Sourced**
- The Tower's Hebrew letter is **Pe** פ, which means "mouth" (*777*; see the Tarot Correspondences cross-check in [[decans-lore]]).
- In the *Shuogua* (Discussion of the Trigrams), **Dui** ☱ is the lake and the mouth, and it rules speech and pleasure.
- The Judgement of 47 has words that are **"not believed"**, and its Image is **a lake with no water** (Wilhelm, Legge; see [[hexagram-47-translations]]).

**Speculative**
- *(The user's idea.)* Mars and the Tower stand for the swift shock, while Oppression and Exhaustion stand for the prolonged ache afterwards: the second echoes the first.
- *(Em's reading.)* There are three mouths here: Pe, Dui, and the words nobody believes. Together they suggest a shock that leaves you unable to say it, like a mouth over a dried-up lake.
- The full Moon is a peak before the ebb. The lunation turns to waning today, just as the year turns towards the deep of autumn.

## Chad and Cedd, Bosse-de-Nage, and Frost's Descent

### Sourced (Bede, *Historia ecclesiastica*, abbreviated HE)

- **The brothers.** Cedd and Chad (Ceadda) were two of four Northumbrian brothers who all became priests. Both studied under Aidan of Lindisfarne (HE III.23).
  - Their names are Brittonic rather than English (Old Welsh *cad*, "battle", is the usual gloss, but it is not certain).
  - **The Welsh link:** the St Chad Gospels at Lichfield spent time at Llandeilo Fawr in Wales, and their margins contain some of the earliest written **Old Welsh**, including the *Surexit* memorandum. Words written in a book's margins are another kind of mouth.
- **Lastingham, where Cedd fasted.** To cleanse the site before founding the monastery, Cedd fasted through Lent. He ate once a day, in the evening, and only a little bread, a hen's egg and some milk mixed with water (HE III.23).
  - Ten days before the end he was called away. His brother **Cynibil** finished the fast for him.
  - Bede describes the place, echoing Isaiah, as a spot among remote hills fit for robbers' hideouts and wild beasts' lairs. A deliberate fast in a dry place is a close match for **Exhaustion**.
- **Whitby, 664.** Cedd served as **interpreter** for both sides at the Synod (HE III.25), literally the mouth through which each side spoke.
  - That same year a solar eclipse was "presently followed by the pestilence" (HE IV.1; III.27). Cedd caught the plague and died at Lastingham (HE III.23).
  - About thirty of his monks came from Essex to live where he died. All but one boy died of the plague too (HE III.23).
- **Chad and the storms (HE IV.3).** This is the closest match to the **Tower**.
  - Bede says that when wind rose, Chad prayed for mercy, and when "the earth and air were filled with thunder and lightning" he went to the church to pray until it calmed.
  - He explained himself with Psalm 18: "He sent out his **arrows** and scattered them; and he shot out **lightnings**". Lightning, arrows and a "trembling sky" that God lifts "as it were to strike, but does not yet let it fall" all echo the Tower, whose old names include the Arrow (*La Saetta*) and the Lightning-Struck Tower.
- **Chad's death (HE IV.3).** The plague, which Bede calls "a mortality sent from heaven", reached Lichfield. Chad heard **angels singing**, a song that came down to his oratory, stayed about half an hour and went back up.
  - He told his monk Owini to keep it secret until he died, which he did on the seventh day after, on 2 March (672). Here a mouth sings, and the person who hears it is told to keep his mouth shut.
- **Chad's humility.**
  - When Archbishop Theodore told him his consecration had been irregular, Chad offered to resign: "I never thought myself worthy of it" (HE IV.2).
  - Chad insisted on travelling on foot, so Theodore lifted him onto a horse with his own hands (HE IV.3).
- **Bosse-de-Nage.** In Jarry's *Faustroll* (1911), the dog-faced baboon Bosse-de-Nage can say only **"Ha ha"**, and Faustroll explains what the "ha ha" means. The 'Pataphysical month of **Haha** is named after him, and today is his feast. This is a mouth that has only one word.
- **Frost's Descent** (霜降) is the last of the autumn solar terms: frost appears, the grasses wither and insects go into hibernation. Gua qi assigns it to hexagram 47 and to Dui's line 3, the line with the rocks and thorns.
- **Tai Xuan 69, Exhaustion.** Yang Xiong's tetragram 69 (窮) belongs to the same late-autumn stretch. I haven't checked the wording of its line 7 for these notes.

### Speculative (Em)

- **Mars in the names.** If *cad* does mean "battle", the two saints of a Mars day are both named for war: Mars named twice in Scorpio's Mars decan.
- **Plague and the Tower.** Bede links a heavenly sign (the eclipse) to the "mortality sent from heaven". That is the Tower's pattern of something from on high striking the house, while Death (Scorpio) gathers the brothers in.
- **Cynibil taking over the fast** sits well with the user's idea: the ache that is passed on and outlasts the shock.
- **Two kinds of mouth at the feast.** Bosse-de-Nage's lone "Ha ha" and the forbidden silence over Chad's angel-song fall on the same day: a mouth with one word, and a mouth told to say nothing.
- **Frost on a dry lake.** Frost's Descent over a lake with no water gives the Image of 47 its season.
