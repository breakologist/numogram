# St Anthony Mary Claret (1807–1870)

Background for the feast of 24 October (an optional memorial in England & Wales; before 1970 it was kept on 23 October). This is historical research, not numogram material. Quotations are copied from fetched text. *Aut* = Claret's *Autobiography*, cited by paragraph number as the Claretian sites quote it. A translation in square brackets is mine.

## Findings

1. **A weaver who programmed looms.** Wikipedia says he became a weaver at twelve, and at eighteen went to Barcelona "to specialize in his trade as a Jacquard loom programmer". The Claretians' official life gives the age as 17 and opens with the words "Among looms, Antonio Claret lays the foundations of his life." His patronages include "Textile merchants, weavers, savings" (Wikipedia infobox). *Caveat:* the phrase "Jacquard loom programmer" is Wikipedia's modern wording, and its cited source is Callahan, who says only that he was "the son of a Catalan textile manufacturer".
2. **A publisher on an industrial scale.** In 1848 "he founds the Religious Library, a publishing house which in its first eighteen years launches 2,811,100 copies of books, 2,509,500 of small books and 4,249,200 leaflets" (claret.org). Wikipedia says he wrote 144 books and that the Librería Religiosa is "now 'Librería Claret'". It also gives the founding of the congregation as 16 July 1849, "in a cell of the seminary of Vic" with "five priests" (claret.org).
3. **Holguín, 1 February 1856.** The attack came after a sermon for the eve of the Purification. In his own account: "he brought the razor he was holding down upon me with all his might. I had my head down and was touching a handkerchief to my mouth with my right hand, and so, instead of slitting my throat as he had intended, he slashed my face across the left cheek, from the ear to the chin" (*Aut* 575, quoted on claret.org). In the Spanish text the wound cut through to "las mandíbulas superior e inferior" [the upper and lower jaws] (*Aut* 576; I saw this only in a search snippet of a blog transcription). He welcomed it: "the long desired goal of shedding my blood for love of Jesus and Mary" (*Aut* 577).
4. **He forgave his attacker.** "I begged him to grant the man pardon and remove him from the island … I offered to pay the expenses of my assailant's deportation to his birthplace, the island of Tenerife" (*Aut* 583–84). The attacker was Antonio Abad Torres. Jeanne Moisand describes him as "un zapatero de Canarias de treinta y cinco años" [a 35-year-old shoemaker from the Canaries], and says he "escapó del garrote vil, pero pasó diez años de presidio en África" [escaped the garrotte, but served ten years in a prison in Africa].
5. **Why he was attacked: a scholar's reading.** Moisand (Casa de Velázquez) says investigators concluded the motive was personal revenge. Claret had told Torres to leave his concubine or marry her. The wider background was Claret's mass marriages of mixed-race couples, which defied a racialised reading of the law of 1805/1842. In 1853 he reported "10 000 matrimonios celebrados y 40 000 niños legitimados en dos años" [10,000 marriages celebrated and 40,000 children legitimised in two years]. His opponents accused his missionaries of teaching "doctrinas de igualdad entre las clases blancas y la raza de color" [doctrines of equality between the white classes and the coloured race].
6. **"Anti-slavery": how far it holds.** The Claretian life says "He fights against slavery". Moisand is more cautious: "Su acción en el terreno de la esclavitud fue muy modesta: sólo trató de cristianizar a los trabajadores" [His action on slavery was very modest: he only tried to Christianise the workers]. She adds that he "se defendió de forma reiterada de promover la igualdad racial" [repeatedly denied promoting racial equality]. **Do not overstate the abolitionist label.**
7. **He carried the Sacrament within him.** "On August 26, 1861, at 7:00 in the evening while I was at prayer in the Church of the Rosary at La Granja, the Lord granted me the great grace of keeping the sacramental species intact within me and of having the Blessed Sacrament always present, day and night, in my breast" (*Aut* 694, quoted by claret.org). He then thought of erasing the passage, but "the Blessed Virgin Mary told me not to erase it" (*Aut* 700). *Caveat:* I saw that second quotation only in a search snippet from claretianformation.com.
8. **Confessor to the Queen, then exile.** Isabella II chose him as her confessor in 1857. He described himself in Madrid "like a caged bird" (claret.org, paraphrasing). After the revolution of September 1868 he went into exile with the Queen in Paris. At Vatican I "he intervenes passionately in favor of papal infallibility" (claret.org). Pursuers then came to Prades "seeking to arrest him and bring him to Spain for trial", and he was "forced to flee as a criminal" to the Cistercian abbey of Fontfroide near Narbonne. He died there on 24 October 1870 (claret.org).
9. **Miracle claims.** Wikipedia lists levitation, light radiating from him at Mass, the calming of earthquakes and storms, and walking on water. It adds that "Queen Isabella of Spain produced a written statement declaring that she had personally witnessed this phenomenon". *Caveat:* the only source given is a devotional book (Echevarria). Treat all of this as hagiographic claims.
10. **Relics and cult.** His relics were moved to Vic in 1897, "at which time his heart was found incorrupt" (Wikipedia; not checked further). In the Canaries he was nicknamed "el Padrito" and is "co-patron of the diocese of Las Palmas, along with the Virgen del Pino" (claret.org).

## Links to today's calendar

These are **speculative**. I found no source that connects Claret with the Prince of Cups, hexagram 47 or Tai Xuan 69. What follows are my own juxtapositions.

- *Hexagram 47 困 (Oppression/Exhaustion, the lake drained) and exile.* His last months fit the image of a lake drained of its water: "Sick, slandered and persecuted" (claret.org), hunted across the border and dying in hiding. The fit is thematic only.
- *Prince of Cups (a serpent rising from a cup; a figure outwardly calm with hidden depths).* The cup-as-vessel image sits strikingly close to his claim of being a living tabernacle, holding the Host "day and night, in my breast". This is a resonance, not a source.
- *Scorpio / Mars / the 5 of Cups.* The razor wound to the face, and his welcome of it as a "circumcision" ("estas heridas eran como la circuncisión de Jesús", *Aut* 577, seen in a snippet only), echo Mars and Scorpio's blade. The joy he took in the loss reverses the 5 of Cups. Speculative.
- *Weaving and Tai Xuan.* The Jacquard loom was a punched-card machine; the *Tai Xuan Jing* is a ternary combinatorial system. A playful parallel, nothing more.

## Unverified or doubtful

- "Jacquard loom programmer" is Wikipedia's wording; its cited source does not say it.
- Wikipedia says the assailant's death sentence was commuted "to a term in prison". Moisand says ten years' presidio in Africa. *Aut* 584 has Claret asking for deportation to Tenerife. The outcome is not settled.
- claret.org also calls the assailant "Antonio Perez" in one sentence. This is probably an editing slip for Abad Torres.
- The incorrupt heart and the Queen's affidavit rest only on Wikipedia.
- Not fetched: the full *Autobiography* PDF, and the Catholic Encyclopedia article (the New Advent URL I tried gave a different article).
