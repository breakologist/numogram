# St John of Capistrano (1386–1456)

Background for the feast of 23 October. This is historical research, not numogram material. Quotations are copied from fetched text. CE = *Catholic Encyclopedia* (1910); JE = *Jewish Encyclopedia* (1906).

## Findings

1. **From jurist to prisoner.** He studied law at Perugia "under the eminent legist, Pietro de Ubaldis". In 1412 he was made governor of Perugia, where "he set himself against civic corruption and bribery" (CE). Sent as ambassador to the Malatesta in 1416, he was cast into prison, and "It was during this imprisonment that he began to think more seriously about his soul" (CE). A dream of St Francis followed. He had married "a wealthy lady of Perugia", but the marriage was unconsummated, so he received a dispensation and entered the order on 4 October 1416 (CE).
2. **Bernardino's pupil and a crowd preacher.** St Bernardine of Siena taught him theology. "At Brescia on one occasion he preached to a crowd of one hundred and twenty-six thousand people", and "At the time of his preaching all business stopped" (CE). In 1429 he and Bernardino were tried in Rome on charges of heresy over the Holy Name devotion, and were acquitted (CE; Wikipedia). *Bonfires of vanities:* none of the fetched sources mentions them for John. The custom is attested for Bernardino's circle, but I have **not verified** it for Capistrano.
3. **Inquisitor.** As legate or inquisitor "he persecuted the last Fraticelli of Ferrara, the Jesuati of Venice, the Crypto-Jews of Sicily, Moldavia and Poland, and, above all, the Hussites" (Wikipedia, from the 1911 Britannica).
4. **Breslau, 1453, stated plainly.** The JE: "The investigation of the so-called blasphemy was conducted by Capistrano himself. By means of tortures he managed to wring from a few of the victims false confessions … more than forty Jews were burned at the stake in Breslau June 2, 1453. Others, fearing torture, committed suicide, a rabbi, Pinheas, hanged himself. The remainder of the Jews were driven out of the city, while their children of tender age were taken from them and baptized by force." The JE also records that his admirers called him "the scourge of the Judeans". The Breslau chronicler Peter Eschenloer, writing in the 15th century, wrote "Capistranus was selbis dobey, do der nochrichter die juden marterte. Er selbis jm vndirweisunge gabe, wie man sie martirn sulde" [Capistrano was himself present when the executioner tortured the Jews; he himself instructed him how they should be tortured]. *Caveat:* I saw the Eschenloer text only in a search snippet from an RCIN (Polish) article.
5. **How historians are divided.** Bert Roest (*Franciscan Studies* 75, 2017) observes that Catholic scholars "have tended to downplay his anti-Judaism", while scholars of Judaism hold him "responsible for a number of atrocities" (abstract, search snippet). Hofer's 1943 Catholic biography argued that contemporaries never thought him "uncharitable or inhuman". Levy's 2005 encyclopedia calls him "a forerunner of modern antisemitism" (both quoted on Wikipedia). The JE adds that he pressed Casimir IV into revoking Polish Jewish privileges in 1454, which "led to persecutions of the Jews in many Polish towns".
6. **Belgrade, 1456.** He was aged 70. With Hunyadi he raised a largely peasant crusade. The CE says he "led the left wing of the Christian army against the Turks". Wikipedia's account is that the battle grew out of a spontaneous sortie, during which John "found himself surrounded by about 2,000 peasant levymen" and led them across the Sava. He survived the battle but died of plague at Ilok on 23 October 1456. His coat of arms shows "a sword piercing a crescent moon" (Wikipedia). He is patron of jurists and military chaplains.
7. **The swallows of Capistrano.** Wikipedia: "Tradition has it that the main flock arrives on March 19 (Saint Joseph's Day), and flies south on Saint John's Day, October 23." So the birds leave on this saint's own feast. The Mission credits Fr St John O'Sullivan with starting the celebration "in the 1920s". It retells his story of a shopkeeper knocking down nests and his reply: "Come on swallows, I'll give you shelter. Come to the Mission." The present state: the Cliff Swallow Project says "there have been few natural nesting attempts at the Mission during the last 20 years". Plaster nests on an artificial wall were unveiled in 2016, and "have not yet been occupied", though "in 2017 two pairs of cliff swallows nested elsewhere at the Mission". The festival still ran in 2026 (the Mission's 2026 event page).

## Links to the 23 October calendar

There is **one sourced point**. Tradition makes his feast the day the swallows depart, which turns 23 October into a day of leaving, in folklore and not just in the liturgy (Wikipedia, as above). Everything else here is **speculative**, my own juxtaposition, and no source links him with the cards or hexagrams:

- *Mars, Scorpio and the Tower.* A friar who led troops at 70, and whose arms show a sword through a crescent. The Mars and martial fit is obvious, but it is thematic only.
- *Death, Tai Xuan 69 Exhaustion and hexagram 47.* He died of plague in the exhausted camp after the victory: triumph followed by depletion. The lake drained.
- *5 of Cups (grieving over spilt cups).* The empty nesting wall at the Mission, and the swallows that no longer come, make a ready emblem of loss. So, more darkly, does Breslau, which no reading of the cards should romanticise.

## Unverified or doubtful

- The bonfires of vanities have not been checked for John; they are better documented for Bernardino.
- Eschenloer's wording, and the Roest abstract, were seen only in search snippets.
- The CE gives his birth as 1385; Wikipedia and the JE give 1386.
- The pre-1970 feast was 28 March, not 23 October (Wikipedia). The swallows' "St John's Day, October 23" therefore reflects the modern date, or else a local custom. The Mission's own page says only that the birds migrate "in October". This is unresolved.
