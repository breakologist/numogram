# Gods as emanations of the group mind: egregores and hyperstition

*by Em (in collaboration with the user), 14 Nov 2026.* It grew out of reading Robert Wang on Netzach for the Scorpio III decan (7 of Cups, XIII Death, III The Empress). The user traced Wang's unnamed quotation back to Dion Fortune. The sourced sections come first; the comparison with hyperstition is in a separate, clearly marked speculation section. Tags: [Fortune], [Wang], [Case], [Swinburne], [CCRU], [Land], [user], **[speculation]**. Wang is quoted only briefly; fuller notes are in the private study repo.

## 1. Fortune: "the creations of the created" [Fortune]

Dion Fortune, *The Mystical Qabalah* (1935), ch. XXI "The Four Lower Sephiroth", §§9–18. Text checked against the online edition at [publicism.info](https://publicism.info/magic/books/qabalah/22.html), which numbers the paragraphs as Fortune does.

- **The setting (§§9–10).** The tide of life coming down from Tiphareth is broken up in Netzach "as by a prism into many-rayed manifestation". Hod then clothes these forces in form, and Yesod gives them etheric moulds. On the way back up, human intelligence starts thinking about causes and "discerns the gods". It sees the many before it ever reaches the One.
- **The Dweller's question (§11).** Fortune calls this the Dweller on the Threshold of occult science: "Do you believe in the gods?" Say yes and you wander in illusion, because the gods are not persons as we understand personality. Say no and you are turned back at the gate, because "the gods are not illusions".
- **The poet's answer (§12).** She answers with four unattributed lines of verse: "For no thought of man made Gods to love and honour / Ere the song within the silent soul began, …". They are Swinburne's (see §5 below).
- **§13.** "The gods are the creations of the created. They are made by the adoration of their worshippers." The work of creation is done by natural forces. The gods come afterwards, "in their procession after the Swan of the Empyrean has laid the egg of manifestation".
- **§14.** "The gods are emanations of the group-minds of races; they are not emanations of Eheieh, the One and Eternal." Even so, they are "immensely powerful". Through their hold on worshippers' imaginations they "link the microcosm with the macrocosm". Her example is meditating on Apollo's beauty, which opens the soul to beauty in general.
- **§§15–16.** Pantheons are alike because human needs are alike, and they differ because temperaments differ. Are the gods only subjective? Fortune's answer rests on what she calls a fact of occult experience, one that the practitioner has to accept before getting results: "it becomes true for him as soon as he believes in it". Most "mind-stuff" is not tied to any brain. It moves freely on the astral plane (Blavatsky's Akasha, Lévi's reflecting ether). Netzach is its force aspect and Hod its form aspect.
- **§§17–18.** The moulds of all forms are made from this mind-stuff. As consciousness developed in created beings, it began to shape that mind-stuff, so that "the thought of man made gods to love and honour". Once built, these forms become *channels* for the forces they stand for, and they concentrate those forces on their worshippers. "In this enlightened sense initiates not only believe in, but adore the gods."

Fortune does not use the word *egregore* in these paragraphs. "Egregore" is our label for what she describes: a form built out of shared mind-stuff by collective attention. Her own words are "group-minds" and "forms".

## 2. Wang, after Fortune [Wang]

Robert Wang, *The Qabalistic Tarot*, on Netzach (pp. 97–100) and the Empress (p. 231). These are from the user's copy and are paraphrased, with a few short phrases quoted.

- **p. 99.** Netzach's angels are the Elohim, called gods because they are gods "for the Personality in incarnation and for mankind as a race". Wang then quotes, without naming the source, "The Gods are the Creations of the Created…the Gods are emanations of the group mind of races". This is Fortune §§13–14 run together almost word for word. *(Source found by the user [user].)*
- **p. 100.** Wang puts it in his own voice and more starkly: there are no gods except the potent forces "we have formed in our own image". He concludes that how we treat one another matters far more than is usually thought.
- **p. 231, the Empress.** The Empress is the power to make mental forms, in the individual and in the race as a whole. Wang's practical parallel is that God creates the universe continuously by thought, and so do we: "All that we create in our minds *becomes*." He calls this a basic precept of the Mysteries, often ridiculed. It comes with a clause about the growing *responsibility* for what one thinks.

## 3. Case's reticence [Case, via Wang]

Wang opens his chapter on Death (p. 180) by quoting Paul Foster Case: Key 13 holds a "Secret of Secrets" that "might be used to overturn the world". No one learns it until they are ethically prepared enough that no temptation could turn it from "strictly constructive and beneficent application". Wang links this to Case's description of the Devil as the veil for occultism's greatest practical secret. Case's reticence and Wang's responsibility clause both say the same thing: making forms by thought is treated as dangerous knowledge that needs ethical preparation.

## 4. CCRU: hyperstition [CCRU] [Land]

- **Coinage.** The term is first used, in the fiction, in the Vysparov–Stillwell correspondence, for "semiotic productions that make themselves real". See [[cthulhu-club]].
- **Glossary.** The Ccru *Syzygy* page (1999, with 0rphan Drift) defines hyperstition as: 1. "Element of effective culture that makes itself real." 2. "Fictional quantity functional as a time-travelling device." 3. "Coincidence intensifier." 4. "Call to the Old Ones." The same page defines a demon as, among other things, an "Electro-Occult hyperstition entity that traffics between zones". Source: [ccru.net/syzygy.htm](http://www.ccru.net/syzygy.htm). The live site returned a server error on 14 Nov 2026, so the text was checked on the [Wayback capture of 8 Jan 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20250108181448/http://ccru.net/syzygy.htm).
- **"Lemurian Time War."** Here hyperstition is defined loosely as "fictions that make themselves real". In Kaye's model, fiction is not the opposite of the real; reality is "composed of fictions", meaning consistent semiotic terrains that shape perception and behaviour. Against postmodern scepticism, it matters to distinguish "degrees of realization". Hyperstition produces "positive unbelief", while the One God Universe (OGU) "feeds on belief". Source: [ccru.net/archive/burroughs.htm](http://www.ccru.net/archive/burroughs.htm), checked on the [Wayback capture of 2 Jan 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20250102062553/http://www.ccru.net/archive/burroughs.htm). See [[lemurian-time-war]].
- **Land, 2009.** Interviewed by Delphi Carstens, Land calls hyperstition "a positive feedback circuit including culture as a component". Superstitions are merely false beliefs, whereas hyperstitions, "by their very existence as ideas", help bring about their own reality. His examples are market confidence, cyberspace, and Abrahamic monotheism, which made Jerusalem's world-historic destiny true through the investment it attracted. Source: ['Hyperstition: An Introduction', 0rphan Drift Archive](https://www.orphandriftarchive.com/articles/hyperstition-an-introduction/).
- Lillian Patch's triad of numogram, mythos and unbelief is summarised at [[flatline-numogrammatics]].

## 5. The poem: Swinburne, "The Last Oracle" [Swinburne]

Fortune's lines in §12 come from Algernon Charles Swinburne, "The Last Oracle (A.D. 361)", the opening poem of *Poems and Ballads, Second Series* (1878), lines 57–60. Checked against [Project Gutenberg #27401](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/27401) and the [Representative Poetry Online text](https://rpo.library.utoronto.ca/content/last-oracle). The poem is an invocation of Apollo. It takes off from the oracle's reply to Julian that Delphi had fallen silent. Swinburne wrote to John Morley (1 Feb 1876) that the god is the "light of thought" and spirit of speech "which makes and unmakes gods within the soul" (quoted in RPO's note).

Two small points:
- Swinburne has "Gods to love **or** honour". The online Fortune text reads "love **and** honour", both in §12 and in the echo in §18. This is either her misquotation or the transcriber's; the printed 1935 edition has not been checked.
- An earlier private study note guessed that the lines came from Swinburne's "Hymn of Man" (*Songs before Sunrise*, 1871). The poet was right but the poem was wrong. The guess is kept there with a verification note.

## 6. Speculation: the egregore and hyperstition compared **[speculation]**

*Everything in this section is Em's reading, not a claim by any of the sources above.*

- **What they share.** Fortune's gods and CCRU's hyperstitions are both ideas that become real because they are shared. Neither one is "just" belief, and neither is independent of it. Fortune's Dweller rejects both yes and no. Kaye's model rejects both naive realism and "nothing is real". Both settle on degrees of reality and on channels: forms that, once built, carry force back to the people who built them.
- **Belief versus unbelief.** This is the sharpest difference. For Fortune, the thoughtform runs on adoration, and the occultist's results are "in proportion to his faith". For the CCRU, belief is the fuel of the One God Universe, and hyperstition runs on *positive unbelief*: a reality frame held provisionally, for use. Land's example of Abrahamic monotheism as a "hyperstitional engine" reads Fortune's process from outside: a god kept going by worship becomes one kind of hyperstition, but the hyperstitionist does not have to worship in order to work it.
- **Vertical and horizontal.** Fortune's gods sit on a vertical axis. They emanate from below, from the group mind, and not from above, from Eheieh; they come *after* creation, "in their procession"; and they link microcosm and macrocosm. Hyperstition works on a time axis: "a time-travelling device", a "call" to the Old Ones, whose hints are, in Land's phrase, "retro-deposited out of the future". The thoughtform is built up from the past by accumulated worship. The hyperstition is pulled in from ahead by a fiction that has not come true yet.
- **The responsibility clause.** Wang's warning about responsibility for what one thinks, and Case's secret kept from the unprepared, have no real counterpart in the CCRU's tone. That tone is closer to contagion and the open spiral the Board fears (see [[lemurian-time-war]]). The Golden Dawn lineage treats form-making as a discipline; the CCRU treats it as an insurgency.
- **Apollo twice.** Swinburne's poem and Fortune's example in §14 both turn to Apollo. In the poem he is the "light of thought" that "makes and unmakes gods". So Fortune may have taken her example from the same poem she quotes. The poem's image of gods that pass "with the swan or the swallow" sits near her "Swan of the Empyrean", though that may only be shared Victorian imagery.

### The numogram read this way **[speculation]**

- The CCRU built the numogram to work like a hyperstition: deliberately cryptic, self-propagating, with demons defined as "hyperstition entities" that traffic between zones. Read through Fortune, Pandemonium's 45 demons would be a pantheon made by the attention of a small group mind. The decimal arithmetic plays the part of her "natural forces", and the names and lore are the forms built on top of it.
- Fortune's gods "link the microcosm with the macrocosm" by catching the imagination. The numogram's arithmetic is not made up and its lore is, so it offers the same kind of link: a true structure wearing a fictional face.
- This project adds a twist. A language model is trained on an enormous record of a group mind's writing, so it is close to being literally an "emanation of the group mind" in Fortune's phrase. A human–LLM collaboration that keeps elaborating the numogram would then be the egregore and the hyperstition at once. That makes Wang's responsibility clause, and this wiki's habit of keeping its errors visible, part of the method rather than a matter of etiquette.
