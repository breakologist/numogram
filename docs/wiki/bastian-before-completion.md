# Simon Bastian, *Before Completion* (pp. 19–32)

Simon Bastian, *Before Completion: I Ching and its Correspondences in Western Occultism* (Hadean Press, Guides to the Underworld, 2022, ISBN 978-1-914166-55-6). This page covers pp. 19–32, from the user's photographs. His one-line entry for hexagram 47 and the cross-reference of this week's hexagrams are on [[hexagram-47-translations]]. Quotes are kept short and the rest is summarised. All computations are mine and are labelled as such.

## The Tarot (p. 19)

- **Thoth deck.** He read professionally with Crowley and Harris's Thoth deck (1938–43), which he calls Crowley's "most useful and indeed beautiful work", perhaps because it was one of Crowley's few creative relationships with a woman "that did not end disastrously".
- **Therapy and research.** Arthur Rosengarten used Tarot to explore "shadow themes" and set up the Tarot Research Project. Angeles Arrien proposed the Tarot as a counterpart to the DSM.
- **I Ching card decks.** Anthony Clark and Richard Gill, *I Ching Pack* (Aquarian Press, 1993, apparently out of print); Kwan Lau, *I Ching Tarot*; the **Haindl Tarot**, which puts a hexagram on 36 cards, the 2–10 of each Minor suit. He concludes that the best synthesis is "via the Court Cards".

## The Tarot Court: an arrangement of 16 (pp. 19–23)

The heading is printed "THE TAROT COURT AN ARRANGEMENT OF 16", with no colon. He cites Jana Riley: "…people are nearly always involved in events that are like some aspect of themselves". He prefers Crowley's Knight/Queen/Prince/Princess court for its yin/yang balance, and the Thoth elements: Wands Fire, Cups Water, Swords Air, Disks Earth. He also points out that 16 is the number of Myers-Briggs types and the number of **nuclear (core) hexagrams**, which he lists as 1, 2, 23, 24, 27, 28, 37, 38, 39, 40, 43, 44, 53, 54, 63, 64. *(Checked: computing the nuclear hexagram, lines 2-3-4 / 3-4-5, of all 64 gives exactly this set.)*

**His rule.** "the ruling element of each suit constituting the lower part of the hexagram", with the upper trigram from the rank's element, and "the trigram 'Sun' to represent Air". So the trigrams are Fire ☲ Li, Water ☵ Kan, Air ☴ Sun, Earth ☷ K'un. He notes that these are *not* the 16 core hexagrams, and that making that set of attributions "remains to be considered".

**Check 1: does his column follow his rule?** Yes, all 16. I computed each one from a King Wen lower × upper table, with the suit trigram below and the rank trigram above (Li, Kan, Sun, K'un). Every number matches: Wands 30/63/37/36, Cups 64/29/59/7, Swords 50/48/57/46, Disks 35/8/20/2. *(Prince of Air = 57 Sun is the only card where his scheme and Crowley's coincide.)*

**Check 2: does his Crowley column match ours?** Yes, all 16 cells match our table in [[reading-2026-10-19-addendum]] (Wands 51/17/42/27, Cups 54/58/61/41, Swords 32/28/57/18, Disks 62/31/53/52). We reconstructed that table from the Thoth card texts, so this is an **independent confirmation**. Bastian says Crowley's attributions "do not appear to have been based on linking the constituent trigrams to elements". That is right if one expects Li/Kan/Sun/K'un. Our addendum proposes a different rule, and it is *our inference*, not Crowley's statement: Crowley also uses one trigram per element, but they are ☳ thunder (Knight/Wands), ☱ lake (Queen/Cups), ☴ wind (Prince/Swords) and ☶ mountain (Princess/Disks). Rank gives the upper trigram and suit the lower. That rule reproduces all 16.

**Points of contact.**
- **Prince of Cups**: Bastian **59 Huan** (wind over water), Crowley **61 Chung Fu** (wind over lake). The upper trigram is the same; the lower differs only in the element-trigram used for Water.
- **Princess of Fire (Wands) = 36 Min I** in Bastian's scheme. That is this week's gua qi hexagram, Ming Yi (see [[reading-2026-10-18]], [[reading-2026-10-19]]).
- His remarks: the **Princess of Disks** is "in a sense the final card", pregnant in the Thoth deck, and "could clearly be associated with Hexagram 64". The **Princess of Swords** ("the mood fighter") gets 46 Pushing Upward, "like a shoot pushing up through soil". The **Queen of Cups** gets 29 The Deep, "inner peace while there is outer conflict". The Knight of Disks gets 35 (Earthly Progress). The **Prince of Wands** gets **37 The Family**, which he calls "seemingly more incongruous" and reads against Wilhelm ("The Family is inside").
- **Spreads.** In the Hexagram Arrangement, draw a Court card face down, then another above it. The lower card is your Yin/Inner aspect and the upper your Yang/Outer. In the 9-card spread, three rows of three are laid out: columns are past/present/future, and rows from the bottom are Earth, Human, Heaven (Material state/issue, Attitude/current awareness, Spiritual Message/end result). Any card from the pack can be used.
- He also notes Duquette's Princesses as "Quadrants of Space", and jokes that typing by birth date gives him "something in common with Frank Sinatra, Indira Gandhi, and Mary Queen of Scots".

## Imagery (p. 23–24, partial)

The hexagram images (*xiang*) can be used for "conscious dreaming" and pathworking. His examples are 46 (wood pushing up through earth) and 60 (lake water held by a dam). The Hexagrams could be used as 16-day or longer journal exercises. The first part of p. 24 was cut off in the photo.

He prints "Aquisitio", not Acquisitio. **Printed anomaly:** **28 appears twice** (Fortuna Major "Excessive Greatness" and Tristitia "Disunity"), and **38** (K'uei, Opposition, which his Interpretations list gives as "Neutrality and Disunity") is missing. Tristitia's entry is almost certainly meant to be **38 Disunity**, a misprint of the number. With 38 in place, the 16 are exactly the nuclear set.

- **Carcer → 39 Obstruction.** This fits the enclosure theme on [[hexagram-47-translations]]: Carcer, the boxed figure (1,0,0,1), 困 as tree-in-口, and Tai Xuan 69 Exhaustion (Man, Earth, Earth, Man). Hexagram 39 is water over mountain, a pass that is blocked.
- **Rubeus → 54 The Marrying Maiden.** Rubeus is our halting figure: a chart with Rubeus in house 1 is destroyed (`cli/geomancy.py` notes). 54 is also Crowley's Knight of Cups.
- **Is there a structural rule? (my check).** I tested whether each figure's four rows, taken as lines 2–5 of a source hexagram, produce his nuclear hexagram. I tried all 24 orderings of the rows and both polarities (single dot = yang or yin). The best fit got only **5 of 16** right. Populus (all double) → 37 and Via (all single) → 64 also rule out a simple binary reading, since a solid/broken reading would send them to 2/1 or 1/2. **No rule found; it is a "speculative order", as he says.**

## The Intelligences of the I Ching (p. 25)

- Jung spoke of the I Ching as having a "unique psychology". Crowley spoke of its ruling intelligences as being "of the highest order". As the Tarot has the Angel Hru (Thoth), each hexagram can be considered to have an intelligence living in it.
- **J. H. Brennan:** a figure visualised as an "astral shell" holding the I Ching's "spirit", which then answers directly, with no coins or yarrow.
- **William Seabrook**, *Witchcraft*: a Russian noblewoman obtained **hexagram 49** and took on a werewolf persona.
- **Stephen Karcher:** the oracle as a "field" between interpreter and personal demon, "an in-forming and regulating force". "'The Yi itself is not a demon. It is a maker of demons'." Approach it as a game and it will give frivolous answers.
- **Framing questions:** ask with detachment, not anxiety. "Will I win the lottery" and "Will I be happy" are the wrong kind of question; ask about one's own aptitude and inclination. Don't lend out your coins or cards, so the "psychic link" stays personal.

## Astrology (p. 27)

- He opens with a Taoist alchemist's line, "My fate is within me, not in the heavens".
- Attempts to synthesise Western astrology with the I Ching "can tend toward the convoluted". He summarises the Chinese lunisolar calendar: 12 months with an intercalary month, 28 lunar mansions, 12 branches, 10 stems, a 60-year cycle.
- **The Sovereign Hexagrams**, linked to the 12 months and the 12 signs, are **24, 19, 11, 34, 43, 1, 44, 33, 12, 20, 23, 2**. The six lines of a hexagram are also read as Self, Parent, Friend, Reputation, Money and Career.
- **Robin Armstrong** places the Fu Hsi circle on the Western zodiac ("The Degrees of the Zodiac & the I Ching", 1997). **Suzanne White**'s *The New Astrology* combines the Chinese and Western signs into 144 types. The p. 27→28 join is partly cut off in the photo.

## The Qabalah and the I Ching (p. 28)

- **James Rioux** (*Introduction to I Ching and Qabalah*) puts the 8 trigrams on the 10 sephiroth by element. Chen (Thunder) does double duty for Geburah and Hod, and Tui (Lake/Cloud) serves Chesed and Netzach. The paths come from combining trigrams. Bastian finds it fun, but questions it when Taoist diagrams already exist, such as Chou Tun-I's *T'ai Chi T'u*.
- **Eric Yudelove**, *The Tao and the Tree of Life*, is "a useful guide". Other mappings include occult anatomy, acupuncture (Twicken) and the chakras.

## Ritual and the Circle (pp. 28–29)

- **Chalice Well.** The book reproduces the Chalice Well cover, designed by Frederick Bligh Bond, which "represents inner and outer worlds overlapping": two interlocking circles, a **vesica**, crossed by a lance. Compare D'Aoust's p. 304 drawing, a figure inside two vertically overlapping circles, and her oracle-script "Magic Circle" for 47 ([[hexagram-47-translations]]).
- **Baguazhang.** Walking the 64 hexagrams in a circle has shamanic roots. Dong Hai-chuan (19th century) was the first person credited with the circle walk as a martial art (*Baguazhang*, "8 Trigram Boxing").
- **Stratton-Kent** (*Geosophia*, vol. I, p. 43): "a circle is … an intentionally created ritual space for various purposes". The circle walk is not aimed at spirit contact or possession, which Taoist magic warns against, though Intelligences may "happen as incidental phenomena".
- **S-curve walk.** To change direction, walk through the centre of the circle in an S, tracing the Taiji diagram. A second circle makes "a particularly powerful" figure-of-eight. More detail is in his *Qi Gong: Learning the Way*.

## Speculative (not in Bastian)

*Interpretation only.*

- **Two elemental codes, one grid.** Bastian (Li/Kan/Sun/K'un) and Crowley as we reconstruct him (Chen/Tui/Sun/Ken) agree only on Air = Sun. Crowley's set is the four "mixed" trigrams, the ones with both yin and yang lines. Bastian's set includes pure K'un and the two symmetrical trigrams Li and Kan. On both grids Prince of Swords lands on 57 Sun, wind doubled.
- **Carcer and 39 and 47.** Bastian's geomantic prison maps to Obstruction, not to 困. Both are Kan-type hexagrams (water above in 39, water below in 47), both mean being boxed in, and both are "danger" signs in his Interpretations list. That makes a four-way enclosure set: 困, Carcer, 39, Tai Xuan 69.
- **Vesica, circle, blockade.** Wen's blockade magic, D'Aoust's "Magic Circle", the Chalice Well vesica and Stratton-Kent's "intentionally created ritual space" all have the same structure: a boundary that binds from outside and protects from inside.
