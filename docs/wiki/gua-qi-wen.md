# Gua qi in Wen, *I Ching, the Oracle* (ch. 3, pp. 45–57)

This page summarises Wen's chapter section "Gua Qi: Theoretical Study of Yin and Yang", from the user's photographs, and checks it against our `cli/calendar/numogram_calendar.py`. Quotes are kept short and the rest is summarised. See also [[wen-oracle-leads]], [[bastian-before-completion]] and [[hexagram-47-translations]].

## Summary

- **School of Mystery (p. 45).** Han Kangbo 韓康伯 (AD 332–380), an Eastern Jin metaphysician "from the poor peasant class", was an adept of Xuan Xue 玄學, the School of Mystery. It was "esoteric, and yet it was about using logic", and Wang Bi, the emblem of the Rationalist approach, belonged to it.
- **Gua qi school 卦氣學 (pp. 45–46).** Trigrams are "units of substance" and hexagrams "units of change". Gua qi rose in the Western Han, inspired by the *Yi wei* 易緯. **Meng Xi** 孟喜 had yang lines mark warmth and day and yin lines cold and night. His student **Jing Fang** 京房 (77–37 BC) extended this: the 64 mark seasons, hours and weather, and since human behaviour mirrors Heaven, they also predict personality and luck.
- **Jing Fang's politics (p. 48).** The influence runs both ways. When "people in power were evil", their *qi* caused natural disasters, so the kingdom's disasters were "the direct result of evil rulers". "Jing Fang's theories got him convicted of treason and executed."
- **Fig. 3.6, Gua Qi yin and yang fluctuations.** An S-shaped wave runs from sunrise/spring equinox, over summer solstice/high noon (yang), through autumn equinox/sunset, down to midnight/winter solstice (yin). It is the same S as Bastian's taiji circle-walk turn through the centre ([[bastian-before-completion]], "Ritual and the Circle").
- **Table 3.1, The Twelve Hexagrams of the Son of Heaven 天子卦**, also called the Twelve Sovereign Hexagrams (辟卦) or "ruling hexagrams (君卦)". Jing Fang matched them to the lunar months:

| Lunar month | 11 Dec–Jan | 12 Jan–Feb | 1 Feb–Mar | 2 Mar–Apr | 3 Apr–May | 4 May–Jun | 5 Jun–Jul | 6 Jul–Aug | 7 Aug–Sep | 8 Sep–Oct | 9 Oct–Nov | 10 Nov–Dec |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hexagram | 24 | 19 | 11 | 34 | 43 | 1 | 44 | 33 | 12 | 20 | **23** | 2 |
| Branch / animal / phase | 子 Rat Water | 丑 Ox Earth | 寅 Tiger Wood | 卯 Rabbit Water* | 辰 Dragon Earth | 巳 Snake Fire | 午 Horse Fire | 未 Goat Earth | 申 Monkey Metal | 酉 Rooster Metal | 戌 Dog Earth | 亥 Boar Water |

  *This is as printed. 卯 is conventionally **Wood**; "Water" looks like a slip. The sequence is identical to Bastian's "Sovereign Hexagrams" (24, 19, 11, 34, 43, 1, 44, 33, 12, 20, 23, 2). "Month 11 always corresponds with the winter solstice."
- **Table 3.2, The Four Perfected Hexagrams 四正卦**: 51 Zhen (spring, east), 30 Li (summer, south), 58 Dui (autumn, west), 29 Kan (winter, north). Each line carries one of the 24 solar terms, with dates and a Western sign. Line 1 is the equinox or solstice:
  - **58 Dui:** 1 Qiufen (autumn equinox) Sept 22–24, Libra; **2 Hanlu (cold dew) Oct 8–9, Libra; 3 Shuangjiang (first frost) Oct 23–24, Scorpio**; 4 Lidong Nov 7–8; 5 Xiaoxue Nov 22–23; 6 Daxue Dec 6–8.
  - 29 Kan: Dongzhi (winter solstice), Xiaohan, Dahan, Lichun, Yushui, Jingzhe. 51 Zhen: Chunfen (vernal equinox) … Mangzhong. 30 Li: Xiazhi (summer solstice) … Bailu.
- **The arithmetic (p. 48).** 64 × 6 = 384 lines; remove the 4 × 6 = 24 of the Perfected Hexagrams and 360 remain, the "seasonal arc of the sun". The remaining 60 hexagrams each cover "approximately six days to six days and seven minutes". Each line then "represents approximately one minute of the year", which is a slip (see Checks).
- **Solar and lunar together (p. 50).** At the vernal equinox the code of 51 (solar) and 11 (lunar month 1) "lock into place". "Every date in time has a lunar hexagram … and, in addition, a solar terms hexagram".
- **Table 3.3, the Gua Qi Diagram Attributed to Confucius 孔子卦氣圖.** A Later Heaven square: top row 57, 30, 2; middle row 51, centre, 58; bottom row 52, 29, 1. Lunar months 3&4, 5, 6&7 / 2, 8 / 1&12, 11, 9&10, with the branches around the edge. Wen thinks the attribution to Confucius more likely comes from the Ten Wings.
- **Practicum 3.1, birth month.** Find your sun sign's two lines in Table 3.2 (Wen, a Libra: 58 lines 1–2). Her example is Cancer, 30 lines 1–2. In the "Optional Lunar Month Analysis", her September birth (lunar month 8) gives 20 from Table 3.1 and 58 from Table 3.3.
- **Table 3.4, The Thaumaturgical Order of Trigrams (pp. 54–56).** This is **Wen's own arrangement**. She says so: "I align the summer solstice with the north sector … I align the winter solstice with the Tropic of Capricorn". The **summer** solstice goes north (Kan, Water), because the sun stands overhead at the Tropic of Cancer, and the **winter** solstice south (Li, Fire), by the Tropic of Capricorn and the noon point of a horoscope chart. That inverts the traditional seasons, so the mystic can "raise Fire" in winter and "raise Water" in summer. The outer ring is Xun, Li, Kun / Zhen, Dui / Gen, Kan, Qian. It is meant for ceremony and spell-craft, not natal astrology.
- **Wider practice (pp. 52, 57).** Gua qi is "a branch of East Asian alchemy" and fortune-telling. "trigrams and hexagrams become periodic tables for Taoist mystics to override natural cycles". It is practised in Vietnam, Korea and Japan. In Vietnam it spread under the Lý and Hậu Lê dynasties, and fed Caodaism and spirit mediumship; in Korea, Yi Hwang.

## Checks against `cli/calendar/numogram_calendar.py` (Em, computed)

**(a) Are the four excluded, and the 60 spread?** Yes. `GUAQI` lists 60 hexagrams with no 29/51/30/58 (`CARDINAL = 坎 29, 震 51, 離 30, 兌 58`), each lasting `GUAQI_DAYS = 365.25/60` (6 7/80 d) from the true winter solstice.

**(b) Do the solar terms and lines match Table 3.2?** Yes, the mapping is the same: line = (term index mod 6) + 1, with Kan starting at the winter solstice. The code reports 寒露 Cold Dew → ☱ Dui **line 2** from 8 Oct and 霜降 Frost's Descent → ☱ Dui **line 3** from 23 Oct. I computed the true 2026 term instants with the code's `find_solar_longitude`: **Hanlu 8 Oct 2026 07:28 BST, Shuangjiang 23 Oct 2026 10:39 BST**, Lidong 7 Nov 2026 09:49 GMT. These fall inside Wen's date ranges (Oct 8–9, Oct 23–24). The code uses true 15° terms (定氣) rather than Han mean terms; it is flagged [approx].

**(c) Sovereign month.** Wen's Table 3.1 gives lunar month 9 (Oct–Nov) to **23 Bo** for the whole month. Our code has no month-long sovereign overlay. The 12 sovereigns appear only as the 辟 (sovereign) slots inside the 60-hexagram sequence. 23 Bo is slot #52, 辟, from **28 Oct 2026**, so it lasts about 6 days rather than a month. The two do not conflict. Wen's month-level hexagram for now is 23. Our day-level gua qi hexagram for 22–27 Oct is 47, followed by 23 as the 辟 slot.

**(d) "Each line ≈ one minute of the year": likely a slip.** 6 d 7 min ÷ 6 lines ≈ **1 day per line** (≈ 1.0146 d). It is not one minute. The "seven minutes" also looks off: 365.25/60 = 6.0875 d = 6 d 2 h 6 min, which is the classical "6 days 7/80" (六日七分). Wen seems to read the 7/80 of a day as "seven minutes". Note also that **360 = 60 × 6** lines (one per day of a 360-day year), while **4 × 6 = 24** lines carry the solar terms.

**Built (24 Oct 2026): `--sovereign`.** This was proposed above and, with the user's approval, is now built. The overlay looks up Table 3.1 by the *real* Chinese lunar month: new moons from Meeus ch. 49, months begun on the Beijing date of the new moon, month 11 holding 冬至, and the leap-month rule. For 24 Oct 2026 it gives lunar month 9, day 15, which is **䷖ 23 剝 Bo, 戌 Dog, Earth**. That agrees with Wen's month 9 and replaces the 6-day 辟 slot in the gua qi cycle as the month-level reading. Month 9 runs from 10 Oct to 8 Nov 2026, and month 10 (2 Kun) begins on 9 Nov. Details and checks are in [[lunation-and-mansions]].

## Speculative (Em, not Wen)

*Interpretation only.* Today, 24 Oct 2026, is the first full day of **霜降 First Frost**, which falls on **58 Dui line 3**. The day-level gua qi hexagram is **47 Kun**. Changing line 1 of 47 gives 58 (see [[hexagram-47-translations]], D'Aoust's Shadow 58.1 and Huang's "alternates to Joyful (58)"). So the solar-term hexagram is the first step out of the day's hexagram: the empty lake (47) becomes the doubled lake (58) when the lowest line, the buttocks on the stump, moves. In Wen's Table 3.2, Dui line 3 is also the first Scorpio term, which matches this week's turn from Libra into Scorpio (5 of Cups, Scorpio I).

## Flags

- p. 57 places Caodaism in "thirteenth-century Vietnam" (Trần dynasty). Caodaism is usually dated to its founding in 1926, so this is probably an error or a compression of older roots.
- Table 3.1 prints 卯 Rabbit as **Water**, and the close-up confirms it is printed that way. 卯 is conventionally **Wood**.
- p. 48: "one minute" per line should be about one day (see check d).
