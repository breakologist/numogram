# St Jean de Brébeuf and St Isaac Jogues

*by Em (in collaboration with the user)*, 2026-10-19. Background for [[reading-2026-10-19]] and [[reading-2026-10-19-addendum]] (feast of 19 October). This is historical research, not numogram material. Quotations are copied from fetched text; JR = Thwaites, *Jesuit Relations*; DCB = *Dictionary of Canadian Biography*.

## St Jean de Brébeuf (1593–1649)

1. **His name, "Échon".** The DCB entry is headed "called Échon by the Hurons". Christophe Regnault's report reproduces his torturer's words: "Echon," that is Father de Breboeuf's name in Huron (JR 34). *Caveat:* none of the fetched sources explains what the name means, so I give no gloss.
2. **A late vocation, and a talent for languages.** He joined the Jesuits at 24. According to the DCB he was first chosen for the Hurons "because of his talent for languages". After three years he "knew enough Huron to be able to translate the catechism of the Jesuit Ledesma". The dictionary and grammar were compiled by a team (Jogues and Garnier were part of it), but the work was "above all Brébeuf's work".
3. **An ethnographer.** The DCB calls his 1636 Relation "a unique document that is mentioned in first place in all the monographs concerning the Hurons". It also says his surviving private notes "represent the earliest pages of mystical literature in Canada".
4. **Three vows.** The DCB lists three steps: "in 1631, the promise to serve Christ even to the sacrifice of his life; in 1637–39, the vow never to refuse the grace of martyrdom; in 1645, the vow of perfection."
5. **The great cross, in 1640, during the Neutral mission** (Ragueneau, JR 34): "a great cross appeared to him, which came from the direction of the Iroquois Nations". Asked about it, he said it "was so great that it was adequate to hold not only one person, but all of us who were in these countries."
6. **A broken collarbone.** On the way back from the Neutral mission he "fell on the ice while crossing a frozen lake and broke his left clavicle" (DCB). That injury is why he spent 1642–44 in Quebec as procurator.
7. **The mock baptism, 16 March 1649.** Regnault's report (JR 34) describes a renegade Huron he had once baptised. This man said "I am going to baptize thee", then "took a kettle full of boiling water, which he poured over his body three different times, in derision of Holy baptism." Regnault adds that Brébeuf "suffered like a rock, insensible to the fires and the flames, without uttering any cry". *Caveat:* Regnault was not an eyewitness. He examined the body afterwards; the DCB quotes him: "I saw and touched…".
8. **The Jesuits burnt Sainte-Marie themselves.** "On 14 June 1649 they set fire to the building and betook themselves with a few hundred Hurons to the Île Saint-Joseph (Christian Island)" (DCB). The garrison had "only 8 soldiers, 22 donnés and 7 servants".
9. **The Huron Carol.** Its Wendat title is "Jesous Ahatonhia" ("Jesus, he is born"). Wikipedia says it was "written probably in 1642". The familiar English words date from 1926 and are Jesse Edgar Middleton's. They bring in "Gitchi Manitou", "which is not in the original Wendat version". *Caveat:* the attribution to Brébeuf is traditional, and Wikipedia's support for it is a secondary citation (McGee 1985).
10. **The skull reliquary.** Wikipedia says "Brébeuf's family later donated his skull in a silver reliquary", and that in 1984 John Paul II "prayed over Brébeuf's skull" near the Martyrs' Shrine. *Caveat:* this is a Wikipedia lead only; I have not checked it with the shrine or with Quebec.

## St Isaac Jogues (1607–1646)

1. **The first Jesuit from Orléans.** "in October 1624 he was the first Jesuit from Orléans to enter the noviciate at Rouen" (DCB). He was "the fifth of nine children".
2. **His Huron name.** He reached Ihonatiria in 1636 and "was given the name 'Ondessonk' (bird of prey)" (DCB). He also built Fort Sainte-Marie: "The building of Fort Sainte-Marie was entrusted to Father Jogues."
3. **He chose captivity.** At the ambush of 1642 he could have hidden "among very tall and dense reeds; but this thought could never enter my mind", and "Flight seemed horrible to me" (his own account, quoted in the DCB). In captivity he was adopted by an old woman and did "a task reserved for women: gathering wood".
4. **The Dutch got him out.** "The Dutch Calvinists at Fort Orange (Albany) made constant efforts to free him, and at last, when he was about to be burnt to death, induced him to take refuge in a sailing vessel" (*Catholic Encyclopedia*). He was "the first Catholic priest who ever came to Manhattan Island".
5. **Eighteen languages on Manhattan** (*Novum Belgium*, 3 August 1646, JR 28): "the Director General told me that there were men of eighteen different languages". The French reads "dix-huict sortes de langues". The DCB calls it "what may be considered the earliest description of New York".
6. **Unrecognised in Rennes.** He reached the college on 5 January 1644, and "His superiors were unable to recognize him, so transformed was he by his sufferings and mutilations" (DCB). Anne of Austria "insisted upon beholding this martyr".
7. **The Mass indult.** The pope granted it "believing that it was not proper that a martyr for Christ should not be able to offer Christ's blood ('Indignum esset Christi martyrem Christi non bibere sanguinem')" (DCB). The *Catholic Encyclopedia* names the pope as "Urban VII", which is an error for Urban VIII.
8. **Lake George.** "Jogues was the first white man to see Lake George, which he named Saint-Sacrement, as his companion Jean Bourdon noted on his map" (DCB). The *Catholic Encyclopedia* says the Indians called the lake "Horicon".
9. **The box.** He left a box of "warm clothes for the winter, the sacred vessels for masses, and gifts" at the Mohawk village. "mystified by the small box left at the Mohawk village by Jogues, they saw in it the confirmation of their suspicions about the cause of the epidemic, the drought, and the famine" (DCB). He was killed on 18 October; La Lande died "probably the next day".
10. **Kateri Tekakwitha.** She was born in 1656 at Ossernenon, about ten years after Jogues's death there (Wikipedia, "Kateri Tekakwitha", whose infobox gives "1656 Ossernenon"). *Caveat:* the date is approximate, and Wikipedia itself says "around 1656".

## Claims that did not check out, or only partly

- **"His mother did not recognise him."** Not found. The DCB says it was his *superiors* at Rennes. The popular version, in which the rector at Rennes asks him for news of Jogues, does not appear in any of the fetched sources.
- **Brébeuf's size.** No primary source fetched. The DCB's "the giant of the Huron missions" is a later epithet and is figurative. The only reference to his "giant frame" is a 1930 newspaper quoted on Wikipedia.
- **The meaning of "Échon"**: unsourced. **Lacrosse named by Brébeuf**: Wikipedia only, and hedged there ("It is said").
- **The death date.** The DCB gives Brébeuf's death as 16 March 1649. The lead plate found at Sainte-Marie, quoted on Wikipedia, reads "17 de mars".
- **"Urban VII"** in the *Catholic Encyclopedia*: wrong pope, as noted above.
